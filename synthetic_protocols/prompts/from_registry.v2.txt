Requirements:
- Faithfulness: the protocol must describe this trial. Keep the design, arms, interventions, doses and schedules, population and endpoints stated in the record. Where the record is silent, supply realistic detail typical of a protocol for this kind of trial, sponsor and era, and never contradict the record.
- Timing: write the protocol as approved before enrollment began, in the present and future tense ("participants will receive"). Registry records are often updated later and may be written in the past tense or describe later events such as enrollment halts, terminations, parts that were never started, end-of-trial dates or results. Do not include those events, an enrollment or study status, or anything else that would not be known before the first participant enrolled; describe every planned part of the study as planned.
- Eligibility is the most important section. Write numbered inclusion and exclusion criteria at the level of detail of a full sponsor protocol, whose eligibility section typically runs 2,000 to 4,000 words. Use lettered sub-criteria, definitions and explicit exceptions where a protocol would, including: explicit laboratory thresholds with units; acceptable specimen types, assay methods, laboratory accreditation and positivity thresholds for every biomarker; prior-therapy rules (number of prior lines, required or excluded agents, washout periods); central nervous system metastasis rules; and cardiac, infection, organ-function, gastrointestinal, reproductive, radiotherapy and surgery criteria, plus the other standard criteria a protocol for this trial would contain.
- Every criterion in the record must appear, preserving its meaning. Before writing, list each registry criterion and confirm that it maps to at least one numbered protocol criterion.
- Add the criteria implied by the study treatments, including any comparator: for example, histology restrictions, organ-function limits, drug-interaction rules and contraception durations that follow from the agents' approved labels or known toxicities.
- Structure: title page (protocol number, version, date, sponsor), protocol synopsis, schedule of activities table, background and rationale, objectives and endpoints table, study design, study population, study treatments with dose-modification and toxicity-management tables, concomitant medications, study assessments and procedures, adverse-event definitions and reporting, statistical considerations (hypotheses, sample size and its assumptions, analysis populations, interim analyses, multiplicity), data management, ethics and regulatory considerations, and appendices.
- Write every section in full prose and tables at the length of a complete sponsor protocol, which typically exceeds 15,000 words. Do not use placeholders such as "[to be added]" or "TBD", and do not abbreviate with "etc." or "per standard practice" where a protocol would state the rule.
- Consistency: before writing, settle the key parameters (arms, doses, cycle length, visit schedule and windows, washout periods, sample size and statistical assumptions) and use them identically in every section. Check the sample-size and alpha-spending arithmetic.
- Do not include any statement, disclaimer, note or watermark saying the document is synthetic, fictional, reconstructed or AI-generated, and do not comment on the registry record. Write the document as the protocol itself.
- Do not cite specific publications or include a reference list; describe the background evidence in prose.
